# THIN MARGINS — Channel Blueprint

Stick-figure money-education storytelling. Faceless, animated, evergreen.

---

## 0. Reality check before anything else

You asked for a channel that "prints money." Here is the honest framing, because it changes the plan:

- **Tapakapa is a great style reference, not a money reference.** It sits around ~100K subs with AdSense estimated near **$1.5K/month**. Excellent taste, small business. Copying the *look* is smart; copying the *business* is not.
- **The money in this format is at the top.** MagnatesMedia — faceless business/money storytelling — has **1.5M+ subs, 150M+ long-form views, and $1M+ in lifetime AdSense**. That took years and per-video production effort measured in weeks, not hours.
- **The niche choice is right.** Personal finance / investing is the highest-RPM major niche on YouTube: roughly **$15–30 RPM**, versus $2–5 for entertainment. A finance video with 100K views can out-earn a vlog with 1M.
- **The 2026 policy risk is real and it points at exactly this kind of channel.** YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed from "repetitious content" in July 2025) demonetizes **mass-produced or templated content that is easily replicable at scale**. Enforcement has focused on faceless channels with synthetic voice, templated scripts, and volume-first upload schedules. AI *assistance* is fine and disclosed AI content earns comparable RPM. AI *slop* gets a warning, then a 90-day suspension, then permanent YPP removal.

**Strategic conclusion:** this channel wins by being **low-volume and high-craft**, not high-volume and templated. One genuinely good 9-minute video a week beats five auto-generated ones, and it's the only version that survives policy enforcement. Budget realistically: **months 1–4 are unpaid**, monetization unlocks somewhere in months 4–8, and meaningful revenue is a year-one-to-two outcome.

## 2. Name & identity

### Primary: **THIN MARGINS**

Why it works:
- Real finance term (profit margin) — signals the niche instantly.
- Literally describes stick figures: thin lines.
- "Margins" = the margin line on ledger paper, which becomes the entire visual system.
- Two words, easy to say, easy to search, brandable beyond YouTube.

**Handle:** `@thinmargins` — verify availability on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and as a `.com`/`.tv` before you commit. Fallbacks if taken: `@thinmarginstv`, `@watchthinmargins`.

### Backup names, in order
1. **Line Item** — accounting term; stick figures are lines. Very clean.
2. **Net Worthless** — funnier, more viral-leaning, harder to grow into a serious brand.
3. **Paper Thin** — "paper" (money slang) + thin figures. Weakest search signal.

### The mascot: **Marv**

A stick figure in a slightly-too-big emerald tie. Round off-white head, heavy black outline, two small rectangular eyes, no mouth. Marv is the audience surrogate: he makes the money mistake in the video so the viewer doesn't have to. He never speaks — the narrator does. He recurs in every single video, which is what turns a format into a brand.

### Visual system (this is the moat)

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm cream ledger paper `#F4F1E8`, faint pale-blue rule lines |
| Margin line | Thin vertical red `#D64545` on the left — the channel's signature |
| Ink | Near-black `#141414`, thick uniform stroke weight |
| Gains / good | Emerald `#12A150` |
| Losses / bad | Red `#D64545` |
| Emphasis | Rough highlighter yellow `#FFD84D` swipe behind one word |

**Hard rule: only these colors, ever.** A five-color palette used with discipline reads as a brand. Six colors reads as a template.

**Typography:** one heavy condensed sans for titles/thumbnails (Anton, Archivo Black, or Bebas Neue), one clean humanist sans for on-screen labels (Inter or Work Sans). Never more than two.

## 4. Format spec

| Parameter | Spec | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Runtime | **8–12 min** for videos 1–10, stretching to 15–20 once retention is proven | 8+ min enables mid-roll ads; don't pad before you can hold attention |
| Cadence | **1/week, same day, same hour** (Tuesday, 2pm ET) | Sustainable; consistency beats volume; volume triggers policy flags |
| Voice | One narrator, dry and unhurried. If synthetic, use a premium voice and **hand-edit pacing and emphasis** | Flat, unedited TTS over stock visuals is the single clearest inauthentic-content signal |
| Animation | Stick figures on ledger paper, held frames + simple moves. Static frames are allowed if they're *designed* | Motion for its own sake wastes production hours |
| Music | Quiet, sparse, one track per video | Finance viewers bail on loud beds |
| Subtitles | Burned-in for hook only; YouTube CC for the rest | |

### The 6-beat script skeleton (use for every video)

1. **Cold open, 0:00–0:15.** Drop into the most absurd moment of the story. No intro, no "hey guys," no logo sting. State the number that shouldn't be possible.
2. **The promise, 0:15–0:40.** One sentence on what the viewer will understand by the end. This is the retention contract.
3. **The setup, 0:40–3:00.** Who, what, what they wanted. Marv stands in for the viewer.
4. **The turn, 3:00–6:00.** The thing that went wrong, or the mechanism that was always quietly running.
5. **The mechanism, 6:00–9:00.** The actual teaching. Draw the money flow on screen. This is why they'll rewatch and share.
6. **The callback, 9:00–end.** Return to the opening number. Land one takeaway the viewer can act on. Name the next video and why it follows from this one.

**Retention rules:** no channel intro animation, ever. Never say "before we get started." Introduce a visual change (new drawing, new number on screen) every 6–10 seconds. Ask a question at ~60% runtime — questions spike comments and comments spike distribution.

## 5. The first 10 videos

Ordered deliberately: broad-appeal mechanism videos first (they're searchable and never expire), narrative videos in the middle (they build the brand), and the highest-CPM topic last (once you have watch-time history to earn the impressions).

### 1. Where Your Money Actually Goes
- **Thumb:** WHERE YOUR MONEY ACTUALLY GOES *(highlight "ACTUALLY")*
- **Hook:** "You spend a twenty. Within nine days, roughly four cents of it is yours again. Here's the route it takes."
- **Story:** Follow one $20 bill from Marv's wallet through the merchant, the card network's cut, the acquiring bank, payroll, and back into a deposit account.
- **Lesson:** Every transaction has toll booths. Naming them is step one.
- **Why first:** Zero prerequisites, infinitely shareable, and it establishes the visual language of drawing money flow. This is your format's proof of concept.

### 2. Why Your Bank Pays You 0.01%
- **Thumb:** YOUR BANK PAYS YOU 0.01% *(red)*
- **Hook:** "Your bank pays you one cent a year on a hundred dollars. It lends that same hundred out at seven percent. The difference has a name, and it's the name of this channel."
- **Story:** Net interest margin, explained end to end. Marv deposits; the bank multiplies.
- **Lesson:** High-yield savings exists and moving is free. Concrete, actionable, immediate.
- **Why:** On-brand to the point of being the channel's thesis statement. Enormous CPM — banks bid hard on this exact viewer.

### 3. The Man Who Went Broke Winning the Lottery
- **Thumb:** HE WON $16,000,000 *(with a red downward arrow)*
- **Hook:** "He won sixteen million dollars. Four years later he was answering a payphone about a $10,000 debt."
- **Story:** The lottery-curse arc — lump sum, family, lifestyle ratchet, no income-producing assets.
- **Lesson:** A pile of money is not an income. The distinction is the whole ballgame.
- **Why:** Your first pure narrative. Highest ceiling for algorithmic breakout in this ten.

### 4. How a $5 Coffee Costs You $19,000 (And Why That's a Lie)
- **Thumb:** THE $19,000 COFFEE
- **Hook:** "Every finance guru will tell you your coffee is costing you nineteen thousand dollars. They're right about the math and wrong about everything else."
- **Story:** Do the compound-interest math honestly, then demolish the latte-factor framing — small expenses are a rounding error next to housing, transport, and income.
- **Lesson:** Compounding is real; blaming the poor for coffee is not.
- **Why:** Contrarian takes on saturated topics ride the search volume of the topic while standing out. Comment section will be excellent for engagement.

### 5. The Store That Wants You to Get Lost
- **Thumb:** THIS STORE IS A MAZE
- **Hook:** "There is no shortcut through IKEA. That's not bad design. That's about a billion dollars of good design."
- **Story:** Retail choreography — decompression zones, forced paths, anchor pricing, the endowment effect at the sample table.
- **Lesson:** You're not weak-willed. You're being routed.
- **Why:** Broadest-appeal video in the set; brings in non-finance viewers who then get retained by the money content.

### 6. The Cheapest Car I Ever Bought Cost Me the Most
- **Thumb:** THE $3,000 CAR THAT COST $31,000
- **Hook:** "Marv bought a car for three thousand dollars. Over six years it took thirty-one thousand from him. He never missed a payment, because there weren't any."
- **Story:** Total cost of ownership vs. sticker price — depreciation, insurance, repairs, financing, opportunity cost.
- **Lesson:** Price is one line item. Cost is the column.
- **Why:** Automotive advertisers pay extremely well, and the "true cost" framing is a format you can reuse for houses, phones, and pets.

### 7. The 400-Year-Old Scam That Still Works
- **Thumb:** SAME SCAM. 400 YEARS.
- **Hook:** "In 1637 a single flower bulb sold for more than a house. Everyone knows how that ended. Almost nobody notices when it's happening to them."
- **Story:** Tulip mania → South Sea → 1929 → dot-com → 2021. Same five-stage anatomy each time, drawn as one repeating diagram.
- **Lesson:** The bubble checklist, so viewers can score the current one themselves.
- **Why:** The most evergreen video in the set. It gets rediscovered every time a market does something stupid, which is constantly.

### 8. Why Everyone Around You Looks Richer Than They Are
- **Thumb:** THEY'RE NOT RICH. *(highlighted)*
- **Hook:** "Your neighbour's car costs more than his net worth. So does your other neighbour's. This is more common than being solvent."
- **Story:** Debt-funded lifestyle, financed status signalling, the visibility asymmetry — you see spending, never balance sheets.
- **Lesson:** Comparison is calibrated on false data. Wealth is quiet by construction.
- **Why:** Emotionally resonant, highly shareable, and pushes viewers toward the payoff video (#10).

### 9. The Guy Who Bought Two Pizzas for $600 Million
- **Thumb:** THE $600,000,000 PIZZA
- **Hook:** "In 2010 a man paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas. Everyone calls him the biggest idiot in history. He isn't, and the reason why matters more than the pizza."
- **Story:** Bitcoin Pizza Day, then the honest turn — hindsight bias, the fact that no one holds through a 99% drawdown, survivorship bias in every "you should have bought X" story.
- **Lesson:** Opportunity cost is only obvious backwards. Don't build a strategy out of regret.
- **Why:** Crypto search volume with an educational spine, which keeps it advertiser-safe where most crypto content isn't.

### 10. The Slowest Way to Get Rich (And Why It's the Only One That Works)
- **Thumb:** THE BORING WAY TO GET RICH
- **Hook:** "Nine videos ago we started counting the ways money leaks out. Here's the only patch that has ever consistently held. You're going to be annoyed by how boring it is."
- **Story:** Series capstone — index funds, time in market, automation, fee drag. Explicitly calls back to videos 1, 2, 4, and 8.
- **Lesson:** Boring, automatic, and low-fee beats clever almost every time.
- **Why last:** Highest CPM topic in the entire niche (brokerages bid hard), and it converts your first ten videos into a binge-able arc that spikes session watch time.

### Title & thumbnail system

Every title uses one of four shapes: **a number that shouldn't be possible** (#3, #6, #9), **a "why" that implies you've been fooled** (#2, #8), **a contradiction** (#4, #6, #10), or **a flat statement of an unsettling fact** (#1, #5, #7).

Every thumbnail: **four words maximum**, one highlighted word, cream paper background, Marv, one red or green element. Nothing else. The uniformity is the point — after five videos your thumbnail is recognizable at the size of a postage stamp, and that recognition is worth more than any individual clever design.

## 6. Where the money actually comes from

Ranked by realistic contribution in year one:

1. **AdSense** — unlocks at 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours. At $18–25 RPM in this niche, 100K monthly views ≈ $1,800–2,500/month. Getting to reliable 100K months is the whole job.
2. **Sponsorships** — the real money, and available earlier than people think. Finance apps, brokerages, budgeting tools, and courses pay $20–40 CPM, often 2–3× AdSense on the same video. Viable from ~20–30K subs. Vet every sponsor: one predatory fintech read will cost you the audience's trust permanently.
3. **Affiliates** — high-yield savings accounts and brokerages pay $50–200 per funded account. Video #2 and video #10 are natural homes. Disclose every time.
4. **Your own product, month 9+** — a paid "money mechanics" course or template pack sells to this audience specifically because you spent 40 videos proving you explain things well. Highest margin, but do not start here.
5. **Shorts** — treat as a discovery channel, not a revenue one. Cut the single sharpest 45 seconds from each long-form video, post 3/week, always pointing at the full video.

**Do not** monetize through crypto referrals, trading signal groups, or "financial freedom" upsells. Fastest available route to killing a finance channel's credibility and attracting a policy problem.

## 7. 90-day execution plan

**Weeks 1–2 — build the kit.** Lock the name and handle across platforms. Finalize Marv's turnaround (front, 3/4, side) and a pose library of ~20 reusable stances so you're never drawing from scratch. Build the After Effects/Canva template: paper background, margin line, lower-third, chart module, thumbnail frame. Write videos 1–3 completely before animating anything.

**Weeks 3–6 — bank a buffer.** Produce videos 1–4 before publishing anything. Launching with a buffer is the difference between a channel and an abandoned channel; the week your schedule slips is the week the algorithm stops trusting you.

**Weeks 7–14 — publish weekly, videos 1–8.** After each upload, check the retention graph at three points: the 30-second mark (hook working?), the first big drop (what's on screen there — cut it next time), and the 50% point (is your mechanism section holding?). Adjust the *next* script. Don't touch published videos except thumbnails and titles, which you should A/B aggressively.

**Weeks 15–17 — videos 9–10 and review.** By now you'll know which of the four title shapes performs. Videos 11–20 should be 70% variations on your two best performers and 30% new swings.

**The one metric that matters early:** average view duration as a percentage. Above 50% on a 9-minute video means the format works and you should scale it. Below 35% means the problem is the script, not the topic, and adding more videos will not fix it.

### Staying on the right side of the inauthentic-content policy

- Toggle the **altered/synthetic content** disclosure in YouTube Studio when relevant. Disclosed AI content earns comparable RPM — hiding it is all risk, no reward.
- Every video must contain **original research, original framing, or original jokes**. AI drafts a structure; you supply the argument and the specifics.
- **Vary your openings, pacing, and visual layouts** between videos. Identical structure across every upload is literally the definition YouTube uses.
- Never publish a video that is text-to-speech over stock footage with no editing decisions in it. That is the exact pattern being enforced against.
- Keep uploads at 1–2/week. Volume spikes on a faceless finance channel are a flag.
